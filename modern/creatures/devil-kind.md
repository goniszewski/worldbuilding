# Devil-Kind

> Not demons. A civilization's last army, and everything it enslaved on the way down.

## Overview

**Devil-Kind** is the catch-all name for the creatures that came through the gates — "devils", "demons", "the hordes" — and it is wrong in an instructive way. What humanity fought is not a single species of monster. It is the **military and slave economy of a dead civilization**: the Ash-Born's own war-forms, bred and built for the March, plus the **Bound** — beings from worlds the empire conquered before its fall, worked to death in its wars.

The Ash-Born themselves are the template most human witnesses describe as "devil": gaunt, tall, dark-skinned against the ash, with faces that read as either masks or skulls depending on the light. The war-forms are what the empire made of that template — and what it made *out of* other species.

## The War-Forms

| Form | Role | Notes |
|------|------|-------|
| **Chaff** | Vanguard infantry | The bulk of the March. Driven, expendable, relentless. They do not retreat; they are **redistributed**. |
| **Towers** | Siege-breakers | Armored war-hulks, bred and plated to absorb firepower and break walls. Slow; the columns' mobile fortifications. |
| **Kites** | Aerial harriers | Leathern-winged, screeching; they relay signals, shadow columns, and pick off the exposed. |
| **Burrowers** | Tunnelers | The forms that opened the underground gates' approaches; they move through earth the way the Chaff move through terrain. |
| **Choristers** | Command caste | The ones who coordinate the hordes. They speak the [Devil Tongue](../lore/devil-tongue.md) aloud, and the sound carries over distance and through stone. The first captured Chorister was the Babel Initiative's breakthrough. |

### Traits

| Trait | Narrative Effect |
|-------|------------------|
| **March, don't swarm** | Hordes move in columns with a patience that unnerves observers; they treat war as work, not fury. |
| **No supply lines** | War-forms carry what they need for the campaign; a host that cannot find food, water, or slaves on the way **stops** — and stops are when they die. |
| **The Bound do the dying** | The hordes' front ranks are disproportionately Bound — enslaved beings from other worlds, spent like ammunition. |
| **Fear of the sky** | War-forms operate poorly under open sky in daylight on Earth; the dead world's sun never burned, and Earth's does. |
| **Hive economy, not hive mind** | They are coordinated by Choristers, not telepathy; kill the Choristers and the host becomes a crowd. |

## The Bound

The **Bound** are not devils. They are the empire's slave species: beings from conquered worlds, bred or broken into service, spent as labor and front-rank fodder. In the March, the Bound were the first through the gates and the first to die. They come in a dozen shapes, few of them humanoid, and none of them willing.

The Bound are the war's darkest evidence: every one of them is a **world the empire conquered**. The dead world's economy ran on them; the lords still trade them; and the perimeters' policy — capture, identify, and **do not return** — is the closest thing JPC has to a conscience.

## The Ash-Born Themselves

The lords and their retainers are the Ash-Born — the last generation of the empire's own species. They are long-lived, literate, and entirely practical. They do not hate humanity; the classical texts have no word that translates as "hate" for a foreign species, only the slave register. What they feel for humanity is closer to what a starving animal feels for a field of grain: need, without malice, and without mercy.

## Weaknesses

- **Logistics.** A host that cannot be supplied stops, and a stopped host is destroyed. The March was beaten by burning the ground between the gates and the settlements.
- **The sun.** Earth's sun, Earth's open sky, Earth's fire — the war-forms' endurance collapses in conditions the dead world never had.
- **The Choristers.** Remove the coordination, and the horde loses its march. The Scouring's snipers were the war's highest-value units.
- **The lords' politics.** The enemy is not one army. The lords fight each other more than they fight humanity, and a lord's host will abandon a campaign to defend its territory.

## For Story Use

### Themes to Explore
- **The enemy as a civilization** — the hordes are not monsters; they are a species's last army.
- **Slavery as logistics** — the Bound are the war's moral wound and its most damning evidence.
- The **horror of need** — an enemy that does not hate you, and will still consume you.

### Story Hooks
- A Bound creature, captured on the far side, is not a slave species anyone recognizes — and it is not from any world on the empire's maps.
- A Tower found dead in the Ural crater had been **alive for decades** — the war-form is older than the March, and its scars include wounds from a war that predates the empire.
- The first Chorister to sing a human language sings it with a **perfect accent**, and uses it to ask a question no one taught it: *"Where is the one who counts?"*
