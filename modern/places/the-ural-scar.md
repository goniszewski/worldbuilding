# The Ural Scar

> The first gate humanity killed with fire that did not come from the other side.

## Overview

**The Ural Scar** is the crater left by the destruction of the Ural gate on **24 October 2031** — the first use of a nuclear weapon against a portal, and one of the two atomic answers of the Reopening. It lies in the Ural foothills of the Russian Federation, at the site of a ruin-complex the locals had called the Wolf's Door and the archaeologists had never finished dating.

The gate stood inside a ring of standing stones on a forested ridge, above a town that had existed since the seventeenth century. When it woke, the hordes came down the ridge in columns, and the town became the front line of the March. The Russian army held the ridge for six weeks, lost it, took it back, and lost it again — and then the decision was made.

## The Strike

The weapon was a tactical warhead, delivered by air, on the ridge. The gate died. So did the ridge, the standing stones, the forest, and the approaches that had swallowed a brigade's worth of attempts to reach the anchor.

The crater is roughly a kilometer wide, glassed at the center, and permanently contaminated. The town was evacuated permanently; its residents became the first of the Reopening's internal refugees in a country that had fought its way through the March to keep them.

## The Aftermath

The Scar is a no-go zone: radiation, security perimeters, and the sheer uselessness of the ground. It is also a **monument**, in the way that ruins are monuments. JPC's analysts study it; the Babel Initiative was denied access twice before a limited archaeological survey was permitted; the Russian Federation treats it as sovereign territory and national memory.

The Scar's most unsettling feature is the **ring**. The crater's center, where the anchor stood, is not uniformly glassed. There is a ring of fused stone, perfectly circular, that survived the blast — the anchor's footprint, burned into the earth. Every survey has found the same thing: the ring is still there, and it is still warm, years after the weapon.

## For Story Use

### Themes to Explore
- The **atomic threshold** — the moment humanity decided some doors must not exist, whatever the cost.
- **Sovereignty and sacrifice** — a nation destroying part of its own land to save the rest.
- The stubbornness of **anchors** — the gate is dead, but the ring remembers.

### Story Hooks
- The ring's heat has been increasing, fractionally, for nine months. The survey team that reported it was rotated out without explanation, and the readings stopped being published.
- A refugee from the evacuated town claims she saw the columns **stop**, on the last night, and stand still on the ridge as if waiting for something — and she knows now they were not waiting for the weapon.
- The Babel Initiative's limited survey recovers a fragment of script from the blast zone. It is not from the gate's outer bands. It is from the **inner** face, which no survey had ever seen before — and it is not instructions.
