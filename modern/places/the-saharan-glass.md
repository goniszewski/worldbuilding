# The Saharan Glass

> They made a mirror of it, so the sky could watch itself.

## Overview

**The Saharan Glass** is the site of the second — and final — nuclear destruction of a gate: a black-glass plain in the North African sand sea, fused from a stretch of dune country by a weapon delivered on **11 December 2031**.

The gate here had been the worst of the war. It stood beneath the erg, buried in a city that predated every map: an anchor in a collapsed ruin-hall, reached only by a shaft that the hordes themselves had opened on Day One. The Scouring's attempts to reach it were the war's most expensive failure — two assault columns went down the shaft and did not come back, and the hordes that emerged from the site outnumbered every other gate's output combined.

The decision to use a weapon here was made by the joint command, at the request of the regional coalition, after the collapse of the third attempt. It was not unanimous. The debate — whether a gate could be **held** rather than destroyed, whether the city beneath the sand was worth more than the ground above it — is still taught as the closest the coalition came to breaking.

## The Strike

The weapon turned a kilometer of dune into a sheet of black glass. The shaft, the city, and the gate were gone; the erg's surface fused into a single smooth plane that reflects the sky with unsettling fidelity. The Sahara is full of glass — the real deserts are littered with it, from ancient meteorite strikes and older, human fires. This glass is different: it is **one piece**, and it is black, and it is level in a way that no natural surface is level.

The nomads who still cross the region avoid it. They call it the Mirror.

## The Aftermath

The Glass is technically inside the containment zone of a friendly state, guarded by a small permanent force, and visited by JPC survey teams on a schedule that has slipped quietly from quarterly to annual. There is nothing to study: the glass is uniform, the ground beneath it is sealed, and the gate is dead beyond any doubt.

The unease is not about the gate. It is about the **mirror**. On clear nights, the Glass reflects the stars perfectly — and several of the survey reports, written by different observers in different years, use the same phrase without any of them having seen the others' notes: *the reflection is not the sky.*

## For Story Use

### Themes to Explore
- The **last resort used twice** — and the question of whether the second time was necessary, or convenient.
- **Deserts as archives** — the city under the sand is gone; whatever it could have taught died with it.
- The horror of **things that reflect wrong** — a surface that shows what is behind you, not what is above.

### Story Hooks
- A guard at the Glass, on a long rotation, begins photographing the reflection every night. The stars are in the wrong positions — not subtly, and not the same wrong positions twice.
- The regional coalition's archive contains the second assault column's final transmission: sixteen minutes of audio, mostly breathing, ending with a voice saying, in a language no one present knew, *"you are standing on the lid"*.
- A survey team measures the Glass's curvature. It is not flat. It is **very slightly concave**, as if the glass is settling — or as if something beneath it is growing.
