# Gate Delta — the Dune Gate

> The sand does not hold still, and neither does the door.

## Overview

**Gate Delta** — JPC designation **G-04**, known as **the Dune Gate** — lies buried beneath the Empty Quarter, the Rub' al Khali, in southern Saudi Arabia. The anchor is a **ring of not-stone** standing in a buried stratum of ancient riverbed, drowned under tens of metres of sand. It was never seen on Day One: the first warning was a **crack opening in the desert floor**, and the horde that climbed out of it marched north until the heat and the emptiness broke them. The Empty Quarter fought the March better than any army did.

## Why It Is Contained

The anchor is **unreachable**. The dunes migrate; the ring is never in the same place twice. Radar finds it, drills chase it, and the sand erases the work within days. The Scouring's attempt to excavate and demolish became the single worst decision of that campaign: the excavation itself became the emergence point — the horde came up **through the dig** — and the operation was abandoned with the pit refilled by a sandstorm that arrived with suspicious timing.

JPC's position since: the gate is contained by geography. A ring of outposts watches a moving target, and the garrison's standing joke is that they do not garrison a location, they garrison a **migration**.

## Holding It

- **The Ring Outposts** — nine hardened points around a shifting ellipse of survey; drones fly the pattern daily; the anchor is re-located, re-marked, re-watched.
- **The Dig Protocol** — no excavation over a hundred square metres without Denial Command's sign-off. The rule exists because of what happened the first time.
- Nothing has emerged since 2032. The garrison does not find this reassuring; the sand never stops moving.

## For Story Use

### Themes to Explore
- **Containment by environment** — the only gate humanity holds by letting the desert do the work.
- The horror of a **moving door** — a fixed point that refuses to stay fixed.

### Story Hooks
- A storm strips the sand off the anchor for the first time since Day One — and the script bands are **not where the 2031 survey recorded them**. The frame has turned itself. Anchors do not drift. This one has.
- A drone patrol finds **footprints** at the edge of the survey ellipse — leading *toward* the buried gate, not away — in sand photographed bare an hour earlier.
- The Ring Outpost's night watch logs a light under the sand: the anchor, glowing through the dune, in a color no recording from Day One contains.
