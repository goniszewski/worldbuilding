# The Far Side

> A world that died so completely that even its silence has a history.

## Overview

**The Far Side** is the military name for the world behind the gates — the place humanity's myths called Hell. It is a planet, still turning, still holding an atmosphere, but effectively dead: a surface of ash plains, glassed craters, frozen seas, and the ruins of an empire that ran out of everything at once.

Humanity holds two points on it: **Camp Ashfall**, beyond [Gate Alpha](gate-alpha.md), and **Camp Brimstone**, beyond [Gate Bravo](gate-bravo.md). Everything else is the lords' country, and the lords' wars.

## The World

- **The sky** — a dim, reddened sun that never fully warms the surface; a constant high haze of ash that turns days into twilight. At night, the stars are unfamiliar, and there is a **second moon** that the perimeters' orbital analysts cannot fully account for — it is there in every photograph, and it is never quite in the same position twice.
- **The ground** — ash, cinder, and fused glass, with the shapes of old structures rising from it. The empire's ruins are everywhere: walls, towers, foundations, whole districts buried by wind-borne ash. The wind is constant, and it carries a fine grit that gets into everything, including the seals of equipment it should not reach.
- **The weather** — cold, dry, and brutal. Dust storms that last weeks. Sudden collapses of the ash crust over old voids. The air is thin enough that every human on the far side works in a respirator and sleeps under pressure.
- **The warmth** — the dead world is not uniformly cold. There are regions that are still warm, kilometers of ground that radiate heat like a stove left on — the old battlefields, where the weapons that killed the world are still doing their work.

## The Perimeters

### Camp Ashfall (Gate Alpha)
The first settlement, the busiest, and the most defensible: dug into a ridge of fused rock a kilometer from the gate's far mouth. Housing, stores, a field hospital, and a command bunker — the permanent home of [Maj. Katarzyna Nowak's](../characters/katarzyna-nowak.md) garrison and the anchor of the doctrine.

### Camp Brimstone (Gate Bravo)
The smaller, harder settlement, on the edge of the Bone Yards — territory of the Pale Thresher. Camp Brimstone is an outpost in the literal sense: built to be held, built to be observed, and built to be abandoned if the order ever comes. Its garrison rotates faster, drinks worse coffee, and files more reports about the horizon than any other unit in JPC.

## What's Out There

The far side is not empty. The lords' hosts move across it; the Bound work and die across it; and the ruins hold things the empire left behind — records, weapons, machines that no one has touched since the war. The Babel Initiative's expeditions are the most valuable and the most dangerous operations in the theater: every one of them returns with either treasure or nothing.

The far side also has a **population problem** of its own. The perimeters' sensors record movement that is not lords, not hosts, not Bound — figures on the skyline that stand still and watch, at ranges where the ash should hide anything. The garrisons call them **the listeners**, and the standing order is to observe, report, and not approach.

## The Second Moon

The object the perimeters call the second moon is the subject of the most careful file in JPC's far-side archive. It is visible in every sky survey; it has been photographed, tracked, and attempted to be contacted — with no response. Its orbit is real, its origin is unknown, and its position is never quite where the previous orbit said it would be.

The Babel Initiative's classical texts contain a description of a body in the sky of the dead world. The translation is contested, but the word the scholars agree on is **"watchman"**.

## For Story Use

### Themes to Explore
- **Colonization at gunpoint** — humanity's first settlement on another world is a military outpost on a corpse.
- The **ruin as resource** — archaeology as a strategic imperative, and the lords' wars over what the old empire left behind.
- The horror of being **observed** — the listeners, the second moon, and the question of who watches the watchers.

### Story Hooks
- A Babel Initiative expedition brings back a map of the dead world's old transportation network. The network is **still active** — and the routes do not lead to the lords' territories.
- Camp Ashfall's drill strikes a void that is not a cave: a **sealed hall** with the empire's insignia over the door, and a mechanism that begins humming when the drill breaks through.
- The second moon changes course for the first time in recorded observation. It is coming lower.
