# The Reopening

> For ten thousand years the gates slept. On the seventeenth of March, they woke.

## Day One

**17 March 2031.** The first confirmed portal opened at 03:41 UTC inside a ring of standing stones in a forest preserve in the Russian Far East — a site locals had called "the Wolf's Door" and archaeologists had dated, provisionally, to somewhere before recorded time. The second opened beneath a flooded copper mine in Chile. The third was a shaft that tore upward through the floor of the South China Sea, forty kilometres from the nearest shipping lane.

By the end of the week, **forty-seven gates** were confirmed active on every inhabited continent. Most opened in places humans had abandoned or never reached: ruins, caves, trenches, deserts, deep water. The oldest were structures that predated human civilization itself.

For the first three days, the world watched. Then the first horde crested the treeline.

## The March

The creatures came in columns. They did not run — they marched, with a patience that unnerved every soldier who watched them through optics. They ignored roads. They moved toward the nearest human settlements, and where the settlements resisted, they did not pause; they climbed the walls of their own dead.

The spring and summer of 2031 is remembered as **the March**: sieges that became massacres, evacuations that became routs, and then, slowly, the first coordinated stands. The creatures could be killed. That was the discovery that ended the first phase of the war — they bled, they burned, and they died in the same numbers as any army. What they did not do was stop.

By September, the hordes had been halted on every front. The cost was high enough that no government on Earth said the word "victory" out loud.

## The Scouring

With the hordes contained, the gates themselves became the target. A gate could be destroyed if its **anchor** — the structure that held it open — was broken. Demolition teams, naval gunfire, and airstrikes killed gates one by one through October and November of 2031.

Two could not be reached by conventional means. The gate beneath the Ural ruins had repelled every attempt to approach it; the gate under the Saharan sand sea had swallowed two assault columns whole. In October, the Russian Federation destroyed its gate with a tactical nuclear weapon. In December, following the collapse of a third assault on the Saharan site, the joint command did the same.

The craters are still there. The world calls them [the Ural Scar](../places/the-ural-scar.md) and [the Saharan Glass](../places/the-saharan-glass.md).

## The Containment

When the dust settled, **nine gates remained**. All nine had anchors that could not be reached or could not be broken — one stood a kilometre and a half underwater, one in the heart of the Danakil Depression, the rest in places that made either assault or demolition effectively impossible.

On **14 February 2032**, the nations of Earth chartered **Joint Portal Command**: a unified military authority with a single mandate — no gate unguarded, no gate unaccounted for, and enough firepower at every mouth to stop anything that came through, or tried to go in.

## The Crossing

JPC's mandate said *stop everything coming from them*. It did not say anything about going through. The special commands sent through the gates in late 2032 came back with photographs, samples, and casualties — and with the knowledge that the far side was a **place**, a world, not a pit.

In **June 2033**, soldiers moved through [Gate Alpha](../places/gate-alpha.md) in force and secured the first perimeter on the far side. In **September 2033**, [Gate Bravo](../places/gate-bravo.md) followed. The perimeters are held today — the first human settlements on another world, built on the ash of a dead one.

## Why "Reopening"

The name came from the historians, who arrived at the same conclusion the theologians had reached first: this had happened before. The gates were not new. The monsters were not new. Humanity's myths were the memory of previous cycles, worn smooth by retelling — and the current event was simply the latest verse of an old song.

## For Story Use

### Themes to Explore
- The apocalypse as **a staff meeting**: bureaucratic, logistical, and survivable — once the shock wears off.
- The strange intimacy of the March — creatures that march instead of swarm, that treat war as **work**.
- What a world does with a threat it can actually defeat — and the politics of the gates that remain.

### Story Hooks
- A soldier from the first week of the March is now the oldest-serving member of JPC — and the only one who still dreams in the rhythm of the columns.
- The 2031 siege of a small town is still listed as a "training exercise" in one nation's official records.
- A gate the Scouring declared destroyed is back on the active list — same coordinates, same anchor.
