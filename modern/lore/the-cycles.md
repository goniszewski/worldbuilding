# The Cycles

> The end of the world is not a prophecy. It is a schedule.

## The Recurring End

The gates did not appear in 2031. They **reappeared**. Deep within the structures that hold the anchors, every civilization that ever found them left the same marks: scorching, tool marks, and, in a dozen sites, the same carved word in the same unknown script — a word the Babel Initiative now reads as **"closed"**. The gates were closed by someone, repeatedly, for tens of thousands of years. And every time they closed, something had opened them first.

The pattern, reconstructed from archaeology, linguistics, and the memories preserved in scripture:

- Roughly every few thousand years — irregularly, never on a stable rhythm — the gates woke.
- Hordes emerged and marched on the nearest population centers.
- The incursion was eventually driven back, or died out, or was sealed — and the survivors rebuilt, and retold, and the telling became myth.
- The last great cycle faded into the religions of the world: **Hell** as the place the monsters came from, **demons** as the monsters themselves, **angels** as the power that fought them.

Humanity did not forget the cycles. It consecrated them.

## The Silence

At some point — the best estimates place it roughly two thousand years ago, give or take the width of a civilization — the gates stopped waking. The hordes stopped coming. Whatever power had driven the cycles simply... paused.

The Silence is why humanity developed the idea that the gates were metaphor. That the monsters were moral instruction. That Hell was a condition of the soul. For two thousand years, nothing came through, and the world's greatest mythologies became the world's greatest archaeology.

The reason for the Silence was discovered only after the Reopening, on the far side: the civilization that ruled the world behind the gates had been **destroyed** — burned, in a war so thorough that its ruins are still warm in places. The hordes had stopped coming because the empire that sent them no longer existed.

## Why Now

The Babel Initiative's linguists and JPC's analysts agree on the facts and disagree on the meaning. Three theories hold the field:

1. **The clock has no off-switch.** The gates wake on their own rhythm. The war that killed the empire only delayed the schedule — the mechanism kept counting, and 2031 was simply the next appointment.
2. **The remnant woke it.** The hell-lords who now rule the ruins of their world raised the gates deliberately — the first raid of a civilization trying to crawl out of its own grave.
3. **Something else opened them.** The Observers destroyed the empire but did not destroy the gates. Perhaps they wanted them intact. Perhaps they opened them. Humanity has no way to tell the difference between a door that opened and a door that was opened *for* them.

## For Story Use

### Themes to Explore
- **Apocalypse as history** — the end of the world is a recurring weather pattern, not a single event.
- Myth as **corrupted intelligence report** — what else in humanity's scriptures is a fact wearing a costume?
- The horror of a **schedule without an author** — the gates wake whether anyone wills it or not.

### Story Hooks
- A carved gate inscription from the last cycle includes a date — and it is not ancient. It is 2031.
- The "longest silence" in the cycle record is not the recent one. Somewhere in the deep past, a cycle lasted four hundred years — and the hordes that came at its end were not the same as the ones that had come before.
- A mythologist points out that every culture that endured a cycle independently invented the same story: the monsters came from a place of fire, and the ones who stopped them **did not look like us**.
