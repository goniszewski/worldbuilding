# Hell

> Not a place of punishment. A place that was punished.

## What Humanity Thought

Every civilization that endured a cycle described the same place: fire, ash, darkness, torment, chains. A land where the wicked are worked to death by monsters. A kingdom ruled by cruel lords who hunger for the living. Humanity called it **Hell** and assumed it was a moral geography — a fate, not a location.

## What Hell Is

Hell is a **planet**. A distant world, shattered by **weapons of mass destruction** in a war so comprehensive that its surface is a patchwork of glassed plains, cinder fields, and dead seas. Its atmosphere is thin, cold, and bitter with ash. Its oceans are frozen salt and brine. It has no ecology left to speak of — only ruins, and the things that live in them.

The civilization that built Hell is gone — and it took its knowledge with it. What remains are its survivors: the lords who carved up the corpse of their world and now fight each other for what is left — fuel, water, metal, weapons, and, above all, **labor**. They do not farm. They do not build. They **scavenge**, and they **raid** — and they operate the empire's machines the way an heir operates an inheritance: by habit, not by understanding. The gates are the clearest example. Waking an anchor is within the lords' power; explaining one is not. The Reopening was an experiment — their first successful act of reconstruction after the fall — and it worked beyond their hopes.

## Why They Came

The raids on Earth were not conquest. They were logistics — and, at first, an experiment. To the lords, Earth was the **easiest distant world** the old network still reached: a backwater, in the empire's own records, whose previous cycles had been met with older weapons and older fear. They were not prepared for what had changed on the far side of the door — a species that had spent the Silence getting very good at killing things that march.

- **Resources.** The dead world has almost nothing left. Earth has everything: water, biomass, metal, a working biosphere. To the lords, a gate to Earth is a mine with weather.
- **Slaves.** The lords' wars are fought and run on slave labor. The Bound — creatures from worlds the empire conquered before its fall — are worked to death in the ash fields. Fresh stock walks in through the gates.
- **Escape.** Every lord knows the dead world is dying. The gates are the only exit. The war for the gates is, in the end, a war for the last lifeboats.

## Where the Myth Came From

The human image of Hell is not invention. It is **inherited memory** — the reports of previous cycles, retold for thousands of years until the geography became theology:

| Myth | Origin |
|------|--------|
| Fire and brimstone | The glassed plains and burning cinder fields of the dead world |
| Torment and chains | The Bound, worked to death in the ash fields |
| Devils and demons | The Ash-Born themselves, seen through the fear of a Bronze Age soldier |
| The lake of fire | A region of the dead world still warm from the war |
| Angels casting the damned down | The Observers, who burned the empire — remembered as divine justice |
| The devil as a fallen lord | The empire's own rulers, who doomed their world and survived it |

## What It Means Now

The discovery that Hell is a planet changed the war's vocabulary overnight. Theologians re-read their scriptures as witness statements. Commanders stopped calling the far side "the pit" and started calling it [the Far Side](../places/the-far-side.md) — a theater of operations with geography, resources, and a population. And the question no one can answer quietly: if humanity's myth of Hell was a true report, how much of the rest of the myth is also true?

## For Story Use

### Themes to Explore
- **Hell as ecology** — a civilization reduced to scavenging its own corpse; every raid a starvation economy's gamble.
- The moral vertigo of a war against **refugees with weapons** — the lords are monsters, but they are also the last of a murdered people.
- Scripture as **evidence** — the Babel Initiative cross-referencing theology with satellite imagery.

### Story Hooks
- A hell-lord offers to trade: slave labor for clean water. JPC has to decide whether the Bound are prisoners of war or cargo.
- A recovered "hymn" from a captured Chorister turns out to be a **survey map** — and it lists Earth as the third world in a queue.
- An expedition finds a structure on the dead world that is not of the empire's making — and not of the Observers' either. It is older than both.
