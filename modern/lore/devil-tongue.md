# The Devil Tongue

> A language with no word for the future — and its speakers are the ones who keep coming.

## The Language

The **Devil Tongue** is the language of the Ash-Born: the script cut into every gate anchor, spoken by every horde that marched, and written across the ruins of the dead world. It is not related to any human language. It is not related to anything human linguists have ever documented. It predates every living tongue on Earth — the Babel Initiative's working estimate places its earliest attested form before the last Ice Age — and it was already **old** when the first gates were set up.

The Tongue is not one language but a **family** — the empire that built the gates spanned worlds and centuries, and its language evolved, split, and fossilized. What the hordes speak in the field is a degraded war-dialect. What is written on the anchors is the classical form, unchanged for tens of thousands of years: a liturgical script, deliberately frozen, used for exactly the things the empire wanted to last.

## The Shape of It

The Babel Initiative's findings, after three years:

- The script is **phonetic and pictographic at once** — glyphs that can be read as sounds or as ideas, depending on context. The same glyph that means "door" is the sound for "thirst".
- There is **no future tense**. The grammar distinguishes only what was and what is. The closest construction to "will happen" translates literally as "what comes".
- There is no word for **peace**, **home**, or **enough**. There are eleven words for hunger, nine for ash, and a vocabulary of remarkable precision for the machinery of war.
- The classical form has a **register of address** for beings that are not Ash-Born. The Babel Initiative calls it the "slave register" — and it is the register the lords use when speaking to humans.

## Project Babel

The decipherment effort is the **Babel Initiative**, a joint program under JPC Research: linguists, cognitive scientists, and machine-learning teams working on the largest single corpus of unknown script ever recovered — gate inscriptions, captured equipment, and (since the Crossing) the libraries of the dead world's ruins.

The breakthrough came in 2033, when the AI systems began producing translations stable enough to test. The method: a neural model trained on the gate script's **redundancy** — the same inscription appears on every anchor, in the same order, with the same variations — cross-referenced against the war-dialect of captured Choristers, whose vocalizations could be matched to glyphs in real time.

The first verified full sentence was translated on **11 June 2033**, from a captured Chorister, during an interrogation that lasted fourteen hours. The Chorister said:

> *"We are the ash that remembers fire."*

It is not clear whether the sentence was a statement, a prayer, or a threat. It is quoted so often inside JPC that it has become doctrine, a joke, and a dirge at once.

## What It Has Revealed

- The gate inscriptions are **instructions**: how to wake an anchor, how to hold it, how to feed it. The empire treated gates as appliances.
- The classical texts recovered from the far side describe the empire's cosmology: a hierarchy of worlds, a doctrine of **harvest** — and an account of the war with the Observers, written as a history of divine punishment.
- The slave register is used even between lords, in formal contexts. The Babel Initiative's uncomfortable conclusion: the Ash-Born consider everyone not Ash-Born to be, grammatically, **property**.

## The Worry

The Initiative's linguists have a private concern they have not fully briefed. Languages shape thought; the Tongue's lack of a future tense does not stop its speakers from planning — but it does change **how** they plan, and how they perceive the people who speak in futures. Every negotiation with a hell-lord so far has had the same shape: the lords understand *what comes* perfectly well. What they appear incapable of grasping is *what could be*.

The other worry is the reverse. The AI systems that translate the Tongue are fed a language with no word for "enough". No one can prove that exposure to the Tongue's grammar has changed the way the models — or their handlers — think. No one can prove it hasn't.

## For Story Use

### Themes to Explore
- **Language as a weapon** — translation is intelligence; a grammar is a worldview.
- The Sapir-Whorf horror: what does it mean to negotiate with a species that has no future tense?
- AI as **archaeologist** — the decipherment is a machine reading a dead civilization's mail.

### Story Hooks
- A translated inscription turns out not to be instructions but a **receipt** — and the empire it was written for is not the empire that fell.
- A Chorister, asked what the Ash-Born call their own world, gives a word the models translate as "**the bruise**".
- The first human to speak a full sentence of classical Tongue — a Babel Initiative researcher — discovers the language has a **third register**, and something on the far side heard her use it.
