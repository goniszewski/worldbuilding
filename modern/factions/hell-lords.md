# The Hell-Lords

> The empire is dead. The lords are what crawled out of its grave — and they are still fighting over the inheritance.

## Overview

The **hell-lords** are the ruling survivors of the dead world: the warlords who carved the ruins of their empire into fiefs and now fight each other for what remains. They are not a government. There is no capital, no throne, no law that binds them all. There is only territory, and the will to hold it.

Each lord commands a **host**: war-forms, Bound laborers, and whatever machinery of the old empire they can keep fed. Each lord rules a **territory**: a cinder field, a bone yard, a drowned pit, a scrap plain — named for what it is, because the old names died with the empire.

The lords speak the [Devil Tongue](../lore/devil-tongue.md) — the war-dialect among themselves, the classical form for ceremony, and the **slave register** for everyone else. The Babel Initiative has noted, with some discomfort, that the lords use the slave register to address humans even when they mean to be civil.

The list is not exhaustive. JPC's far-side intelligence estimates between nine and fourteen active lords of consequence, with a shifting penumbra of lesser warlords, band leaders, and herders of the Bound. The lords' wars are constant, low-grade, and fought over the things the dead world lacks: fuel, water, metal, and labor.

## The Economy of Ruin

The dead world has no industry to speak of — only **reclamation**. The lords' hosts are built from the carcass of the empire: weapons dug from old battlefields, armor stamped from salvaged hulls, war-forms repaired until repair is all they are. The Bound do the dying.

- **Resources.** Every lord needs what their world no longer has. The gates are the only source of fresh water, biomass, and working metal — which is why the gates are the most fought-over ground in the dead world.
- **Slaves.** The Bound are the economy. Worked to death in the ash fields, spent in the wars, traded like currency. A lord's power is measured in hosts, and a host is measured in how much Bound labor it can burn.
- **The gates.** A gate is a mine, a farm, and an exit. The lords who do not hold a gate are the lords who will die first when the world finally gives out.

## The Lost Knowledge

The lords are the empire's heirs, and like most heirs they have the property and not the plan. The empire's machines still run across the dead world — reclamation rigs, war-frames, the anchors themselves — but the knowledge that built them died with the empire, and the lords keep the inheritance alive by rote: rituals, routines, and half-understood manuals preserved like scripture. The gates are the sharpest example. Waking an anchor is a procedure the lords perform with confidence and do not understand; sealing one is slower, exacting work, and the few who still tend the classical texts guard the procedure jealously. The Reopening was an experiment — the first successful act of reconstruction since the fall — and it succeeded beyond the lords' hopes. That they misjudged what waited on the far side of the door is the war's only comfort.

## Politics

There is no diplomacy among the lords — only **tribute, truce, and betrayal**. Alliances form for a campaign and dissolve at its first success. Every lord's real goal is to leave the dead world, and every lord knows the others know it; the only question is who leaves with a host intact and who leaves as cargo.

The arrival of humanity changed the calculus. The perimeters are a standing insult — a foreign power holding ground on the dead world itself — and the lords' responses have split along old fault lines: attack the perimeters, ignore them, or (as [He-Who-Sifts-Ash](../characters/he-who-sifts-ash.md) has done) **talk to them**.

## The Long View

The Babel Initiative's translations have revealed what the lords already know: the empire that built the dead world believed it was eternal. The war with the Observers proved otherwise. Every lord alive is the heir of that failure, and the weight of it shows in the dead world's politics — the constant warfare, the hoarding, the refusal to build anything that cannot be carried. The lords are not monsters playing at empire. They are the last generation of a civilization that learned, too late, that it was mortal.

## For Story Use

### Themes to Explore
- **Warlord politics** — a fractured enemy that is still, in aggregate, unbeatable.
- The tragedy of **refugees with weapons** — the lords are monsters; they are also the last of a murdered people, and the distinction gets harder to hold.
- The economics of **slave labor at the end of the world** — what a society looks like when it has nothing left to spend but lives.

### Story Hooks
- A minor lord offers the perimeters a deal: **food for water**, the first honest trade in the dead world. The catch is which lord the food was stolen from.
- The Pale Thresher begins selling not scrap but **records** — the empire's archives, recovered from the Bone Yards. The Babel Initiative cannot buy them fast enough, and Denial Command cannot stop them.
- Maw-That-Counts is sighted at the head of a host moving toward Gate Bravo — and it is carrying something that is not a weapon.
