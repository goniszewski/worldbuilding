# The Abyss

> A layered void of power, hunger, and entities older than mortal memory.

# Otchlan (The Abyss)

**Otchlan**, *the Abyss*, is a place—or rather a multi-layered dimension—where mysterious beings of indeterminate consciousness and immense or even infinite power dwell. These beings are known as **Aspects** and **demons**. Contact with the Abyss is possible through magic, which appears to be the only thing connecting these two worlds.

Neither the living nor Aspects/demons can set foot in the other's realm (though some archmagi from Ur are said to have accomplished this), and can only influence the magical forces of the other world in more or less conscious ways.

## The Bulwark

The Abyss operates primarily in one direction: the mortal world —magic—→ |**the Bulwark** (opoka)| ←— the Abyss. The Abyss itself seems "limited" by an impenetrable Bulwark from within. Only Aspects may be capable of "escaping" some of their power through it in the other direction.

> The Bulwark is a semi-permeable barrier that allows magical energy to flow outward but prevents physical or spiritual entities from crossing.

## Demons

Demons, unlike those in most tales, are not obsessed with possessing every living being. Here they don't so much "possess" as "distort" and "adapt" to mages (though the barrier prevents them from doing more than this). They primarily target mages with exceptionally developed magical abilities, understanding of magical principles, and the capacity for sustained contact with the Abyss.

An unprepared mage's contact with a demon can kill or drive them mad. Most mages therefore limit themselves to very brief contacts with demons, being careful not to be too greedy when "asking" for power or assistance.

Demons communicate exclusively through magic—it is their "language"—though beings in the Abyss can "learn" a mage's language or even their thought patterns during first contact, unless the mage restricts access to their consciousness.

### Demon Traits

| Aspect | Demons |
|--------|--------|
| Power | Weaker, unstable |
| Accessibility | More approachable |
| Communication | Via magic only |
| Influence method | "Distortion" and "adaptation" |

## Aspects

Aspects are vastly more powerful and stable than demons—but contacting them is so difficult that they more often establish contact from their side. Aspects are so powerful that they can sometimes pierce the Bulwark surrounding the Abyss and reach a mage. This is commonly described as the mage being "blessed" by a creator or deity.

The better the connection and greater the Aspect's determination, the more influence they can have on the world. In theory, an extremely powerful mage could destroy the entire world, but this remains theoretical—Aspects are inherently "good."

| Aspect | Aspects |
|--------|---------|
| Power | Greater, stable |
| Accessibility | Extremely difficult |
| Communication | Can initiate from their side |
| Influence method | Direct manifestation of will |

## The Fall of the Ancients

Thousands of years ago, the Abyss was far more connected to the world of the living. Demons could far more easily cross into this world through summoning and did so willingly.

Everything changed as a result of a ritual performed by the **Pradawni** (Ancients).

Knowing that magic in its current form could not be effectively controlled and that some group of mages would inevitably cross all boundaries and bring demons to earth, the Ancients—a powerful civilization controlling nearly the entire continent—decided to isolate the Abyss from this world.

The most eminent mages of the Ancients spent decades preparing the ritual until a civil war forced them to put theory into practice. The war raged between loyalists who wanted to restrict access to the Abyss and control magic, and those who wished things to remain as they were—the latter were winning.

The ritual, conducted in an act of desperation when armies controlled by demons ravaged the last remnants of loyalist territories, required more power than originally planned—but succeeded. The Abyss was trapped within a barrier drawing power from the mana wells within the Abyss itself and from the demons whose influence was thereby limited.

Unfortunately, the accompanying explosion of magic led to **mana burning**—something like napalm that destroyed infrastructure and buildings (hence the lack of ruins, except for the undiscovered shelters of the Ancients). This killed all magical beings. The few survivors were too weak and too far apart to rebuild civilization and perished shortly after.

### The Survivors

Some survived. The most eminent loyalists hid in several underground shelters, each surrounded by **mana stabilizers** (screwdrivers with the tip pointing down, rotating on their own axis, maintaining magic at appropriately high levels for "working" with it, but also acting as a "safety" to prevent any magical effects from escaping the designated area) and **mana absorbers** designed to neutralize mana leaks.

Some shelters couldn't withstand the explosion, others were seriously damaged and became death traps, but two—where the Ancients had been in hibernation—survived. After several hundred years, they finally opened. Magic was already stable but far rarer than before.

The survivors, having lost not only their magical abilities but also their memories due to prolonged exposure to mana absorbers, emerged into a new environment. Disoriented with constantly impaired memory centers, they eventually found other survivors and started from scratch.

Thousands of years later, after long migrations trying to find their place in a world whose nature had already healed, they finally encountered fertile and resource-rich lands where they met other intelligent beings—**naters**, humans, and eventually elves.

## The Afterlife

According to most beliefs, one level of the Abyss is reserved for the souls of the dead.

## The Pure Ones

> According to legend, this was the first contact of the **Pure Ones** with magic, which led to the end of the era. Exposed to prolonged magical energy, the soul of **[The First One](../characters/the-first-one.md)** was blessed (or tainted, depending on interpretation) by establishing a connection with the Abyss.

The Aspects, with their indeterminate consciousness, involuntarily (and according to some, deliberately) left an imprint on her soul and granted her the ability to pass into their dimension after death.

Contact with our dimension caused a small piece to tear away from each Aspect. These pieces mixed together and formed the greater and lesser **Demons**—chaotic beings pulled by various forces often aligned with violent emotions from living beings they contact. During contact, the dominant emotion shapes them.

## Structure of the Abyss

| Level | Inhabitants | Description |
|-------|-------------|-------------|
| **Source** | Mana Well | The origin point of all known mana, magic's carrier |
| **First Level** | Aspects | Closest to the source, the largest circle surrounding it. Oversaturated with mana |
| **Second Level*** | Demons | Demons feed on remnants of mana left by Aspects (still far more than mortals possess). Chaos reigns |
| **Third Level*** | Weaker Demons | Inhabited by weaker demons displaced from the second level. They crave mana above all else. The most frequently "visited" by mages |
| **Afterlife** | Souls of the Dead | Here souls fall into eternal sleep. Aspects can restore them to the living world if the body survives, or subject them to reincarnation. Difficult to access from the living plane (too little mana). A place essentially frozen, where time moves slower |
| **Living World (?)** | Living Beings | A purely theoretical level belonging to the living, suggesting the Bulwark did not separate the entire Abyss from us, but only part of it |

> \* Levels two and three are commonly believed to be one, which is an erroneous assumption.
