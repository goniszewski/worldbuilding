# The Divine Begetting

*Cosmological diagram depicting the creation of the sapient races according to ancient theological belief.*

![[creation-diagram.jpg|The Divine Begetting]]

## Overview

This sacred diagram illustrates the most widely held creation belief among the civilizations of the realm. It depicts **The First** — the source of all divine power, neither fully masculine nor feminine, often rendered as a convergence of pure light and primordial shadow — surrounded by their children. Each divine heir reaches outward, and where their hand touches the void, a race is born.

The children do not rule their races. They **serve** them.

---

## The Diagram's Structure

The image shows a hierarchical composition:

| Element | Symbolism |
|---------|-----------|
| Central figure | **The First** — the undivided source, the one before the many |
| Surrounding figures | **The Children** — gods and goddesses, each unique in aspect |
| Radiating hands | The touch that births — patronage made manifest |
| Forms emerging | The first of each sapient race, rising from mist, stone, or starlight |

---

## Key Beliefs

According to this tradition:

1. **Shared parentage** — All sapient races are siblings, children of The First's children
2. **Patronage as sacred duty** — A god-child's purpose is to nurture, not command
3. **Distinct gifts** — Each race carries the imprint of their patron's nature
4. **The First remains** — The source does not act directly; The First's children are intermediaries

---

## Variations

The number of divine children varies by culture and temple:

| Tradition | Children | Source |
|-----------|----------|--------|
| The Sevenfold Covenant | 7 | Elder temples, most widespread |
| The Twelve Moons | 12 | Agricultural societies, lunar calendars |
| The Infinite Hosts | 1 per star | Heretical sects, star-worshippers |
| The Lost Thirteenth | 13 (one erased) | Underground cults, forbidden texts |

## For Writers / Game Designers

This cosmology provides:

- A pantheon structure where each god is tied to a specific race
- Religious tension when a patron deity falls, dies, or disappears
- Quest potential: finding a lost god-child, reconciling warring divine siblings
- Moral complexity: is patronage truly benevolent, or does it create dependency?
- Heresy mechanics: what happens when a race rejects their patron?

**Adventure hooks:**
- A dying god's last heir is hidden among mortals — and the race they patronize is dying with them
- Two races discover their patrons were siblings who quarreled in the dawn age, and the players must find a way to reconcile them
- A heretical sect claims The First had a **thirteenth child**, one erased from every temple — but their descendants still walk the world
- A god-child returns from exile and demands their people choose: the old patron or the new faith they've built without them

---

*Variations of this belief exist across cultures, with some traditions emphasizing cooperation between divine siblings in the creation, while others describe betrayal, abandonment, or divine silence.*
