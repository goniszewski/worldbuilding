# Religious Systems

Several religions and minor denominations exist across the world, often aligned with specific races. The central figure in all faiths is [[pierwsza|The First One]] and her three children.

> **Inspiration:** [[wppl>Zaratusztrianizm]]

---

## Overview

| Religion | Followers | Type | First One Status |
|----------|-----------|------|------------------|
| [[#dorenism|Dorenism]] | Humans (mostly), some elves | Henotheistic | Goddess (reincarnation) |
| [[#oprenism|Oprenism]] | Humans (ex-continental) | Gnostic | Prophet of the Creator |
| [[#arthadal|Arth'Adal]] | Naterians (>90%) | Agnostic | Precursor, mortal |

## Dorenism

*Dominant faith of continental humans; partially followed by elves. Name derived from Albanian "dorë" meaning "hand".*

### Core Beliefs

Dorenism recognizes [[pierwsza|The First One]] (+ her husband who watches over flora and fauna, and advises her somewhat, while she tends to her grandchildren and celestial bodies) as the reincarnation of a goddess who descended personally to this world to stop the prevailing chaos and corruption, then cleanse all lands (it is unspecified exactly from whom — [[pradawni|the Ancients]] or [[demons|demons]]) leaving alive only a group of the chosen.

The devotion and strong faith of these chosen ones convinced the goddess to give this world one more chance, so she left them her children — [[Torr|Tor]], [[Daraster|Daraster]], and [[Lesandra|Lesandra]] (who hold the status of minor gods) along with the dormant gift of magic within them, which was meant to help them do good and guide the chosen.

### The Fall of Tor

Tor eventually turned away from his mother and siblings, refusing to walk the path designated for him, choosing his own way instead — condemning himself to banishment and disfavor.

**In short:** The order considers Tor the prodigal son, who will yet understand his mistake and return (i.e., when the Naterians and other non-believers "convert" to their faith and everything returns to "normal"). All evil in the world is attributed to Tor's absence from the faith's path, creating a void that cannot be filled even through strong faith and devotion of the remaining siblings and their children.

> **Note:** Dwarves, though partly Naterians, are still considered acceptable.

### Key Figures

| Figure | Status | Role |
|--------|--------|------|
| The First One | Goddess | Descended to cleanse the world |
| Her Husband | Deity | Watches over flora and fauna |
| Tor | Minor God | Banished, the prodigal son |
| Daraster | Minor God | Married Lesandra to strengthen devotion |
| Lesandra | Minor Goddess | Stayed on the path |

### Organization & Symbols

- **Institution:** The Order
- **Magic Enforcement:** Paladins
- **Symbol:** Open hand with folded thumb

## Oprenism

*Smaller but significant faith, primarily practiced by humans outside the continent. Name derived from Esperanto "oprensning" meaning "cleansing".*

### Core Beliefs

Oprenizm recognizes the First One only as a prophet of the world's creator god (gender never clearly specified), not his reincarnation in female form.

According to this faith, the First One descended from the Pure — the creator's primordial creation placed on these lands. The Creator loved them so deeply that He decided to give them what they lacked: purpose in life. He chose the First One as His prophet to teach the Pure how to curry His favor (holy transmissions, etc.).

When they complied, the god blessed them with the ability to make their own choices (with the caveat that He must always be first in priorities). He also gifted His prophet with children to lead the Pure further, as thanks for her devotion.

### The Sin of the First One

However, the First One — now having the ability to choose — went to the cave forbidden by the Creator, from which part of His infinite power emanated, and decided on her own, without His permission, to give her children a present: the power no living being should wield — **magic**.

Seeing what happened, the Creator, as punishment for succumbing to her selfish desire, sentenced the First One to death. Yet He knew it was only through the ability to make choices that He gave them that this came to pass.

He allowed the now-ailing First One to give birth to three more children (Tor, Daraster, Lesandra) before taking her from the living world. The First One begged during childbirth for forgiveness and for her children not to be punished — and the god, as a loving father who made a mistake by giving the unprepared Pure the ability to choose, forgave her and invited her to His side (His right hand; His left remains for the First One's children).

He allowed her children to live but never reconciled with the power flowing in their veins, forbidding them from using it. He also declared that only when the living eradicate (or stop using) magic from this world will they be able to gain His favor once more, then turned His gaze from this world, leaving it to itself in hope that this time they will listen.

### Views on Magic and Other Races

**In short:** Magic is evil, unworthy of true adherents of the god, and should be prohibited. In reality, faithful who display magical abilities are separated from their families through meditation, education, and — in more radical denominations/followers — brutal means, even condemning to death mages who refuse to "de-magick."

According to them, dwarves are something worse — similar to the Naterian faith, despite the fact that followers of this religion and Naterians particularly dislike each other. The god, not a goddess, is central. He also didn't create the world — He just placed the Pure in an already existing one, unsuitable for them.

### Organization & Symbols

- **Institution:** The Order
- **Magic Enforcement:** The Inquisition
- **Symbol:** Sword and Torch

## Arth'Adal

*Naterian belief (or rather: worldview). Name derived from Kannada "arthadalli" meaning "reason".*

### Core Beliefs

Arth'Adal recognizes the First One as the first to receive the gift of magic in some unspecified way, and her children (standard), just like in other religions, as precursors of currently living races.

They believe in the existence of [[pradawni|the Ancients]], their disappearance, the Pure, and the First One and her children — but do **not** believe in the existence of a goddess or god. Instead, they believe in a chaotic being (or an unlimited number of them) located in the abyss.

### Tor as the Chosen One

For them, **Tor is the chosen child** of the First One, who could no longer watch Daraster's unworthy and unbecoming behavior — whose pinnacle was his "foul" relationship with their own sister, Lesandra.

Only Tor and his children are worthy to rule this world. According to followers of these beliefs, Daraster is unworthy of the title of Tor's brother, while Lesandra — despite her marriage/relationship with Daraster — still qualifies, because at least she and her children lived somewhat worthy lives and maintained certain purity and integrity (e.g., they help the poor, are quite practical, not as greedy, etc.).

### Views on Races

- **Dwarves:** Considered worse than humans — "subhumans," unworthy of spitting on
- **Humans:** Unworthy, cunning, and selfish
- **Elves:** Somewhat acceptable, but still lesser
- **Naterians:** The superior race

According to them, dwarves are the first Naterians who conspired with the first humans — unthinkable.

### Views on Magic

Magic is **practical and useful**, even dark magic.

### Organization & Symbols

- **Institution:** Council of Beliefs
- **Magic Enforcement:** Tamers, Seeders of Silence
- **Symbol:** Triangle (triangle with a vertical internal line from the upper vertex ending at 3/4 length toward the base)

## Alar

*Elven religion. Name derived from Estonian "alar" meaning "winged".*

### Core Beliefs

The Elves believe that the First One was a goddess who descended to this world to give meaning to the lives of the Pure and show them the right path they should walk.

They consider her children blessed, who led their "flocks" entrusted to them by the goddess along designated paths. But first Daraster — and centuries later, Tor — strayed and deviated from their paths.

### The Three Paths

| Figure | Status | Fate |
|--------|--------|------|
| **Daraster** | Strayed | Chose to "rest" and think about where he should walk |
| **Tor** | Strayed | Continuously seeks the ancient path while walking his own, winding way — hoping to eventually cross the old one |
| **Lesandra** | **Never strayed** | Led her people, the elves, toward glory and blood purity |

### The Blessing of the Goddess

Only Lesandra never left her designated path and led her people, the elves, toward glory and blood purity — for which the goddess rewarded them with greater magical ability than other races and longer lives (up to 350 years), nearly as long as the Pure (blood did mix somewhat; the Pure could live over 500 years).

They await the return of the goddess and Lesandra, who would stand at Her side and help the children of Daraster and Tor finally find the path they have so long "sought" (though Daraster has a longer way to go, having stood still the entire time).

### Elven Attitudes

**In short:** As the only ones who never strayed from the goddess's designated path, they consider themselves the last hope of the world. They do not take kindly to other religions, calling them "lies, convenient excuses, and the work of doubt lurking in the minds of other races."

- **Humans:** They look down on them (though they don't openly admit it)
- **Naterians:** Cold but respectful — treating them almost as equals (with emphasis on "almost")
- **Dwarves:** A curiosity to them

### Organization & Symbols

- **Institution:** Pantheon College
- **Magic Enforcement:** Black Cloaks
- **Symbol:** Wavy circle (drawn with a wavy line)

## GM Notes

### Potential Conflicts

1. **Oprenizm vs. Naterians** — Despite both viewing dwarves negatively, these factions despise each other
2. **Alar vs. Everyone** — Elves' superiority complex creates friction with all races
3. **Dorenizm vs. Oprenizm** — Goddess vs. Prophet dichotomy; theologically opposed
4. **Arth'Adal vs. Magic Users** — Naterians tolerate magic but consider themselves superior

### Campaign Hooks

- A Dorenist paladin discovers evidence that Tor may return — cult or prophecy?
- An Oprenist mage seeks asylum from the Inquisition
- An elven Black Cloak investigates a human mage operating in elven territory
- A Naterian claims to have found proof of the "chaotic beings in the abyss"
