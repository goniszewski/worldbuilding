# Virtuti, Vincere, Gloria et Mori

> A martial maxim praising virtue, victory, glory, and death.

## Translation

| Latin | English |
|-------|---------|
| **Virtuti** | To Virtue / For Courage |
| **Vincere** | To Conquer |
| **Gloria** | Glory |
| **et** | and |
| **Mori** | To Die |

*"For Courage, Conquest, Glory, and Death"*

## Known Appearances

This motto appears in several locations:

- **Ancient ruins** — Inscribed on crumbling walls and forgotten temples
- **Castles** — Carved above doorways and in great halls
- **Heraldry** — Included in family crests and banners

### Visual Presentation

The text is often rendered in distinguished fonts:
- **Medieval blackletter** style
- **Roman inscriptional** capitals
- **Stylized elvish** adaptations

### Hidden Meaning

Some inscriptions are known to contain an additional "smaczek" (nugget): when read **backwards**, the letters form another message or name. The reverse reading has been used as:
- Hidden dedications
- Secret identifications
- Cryptographic markers for secret societies

## Significance

The motto embodies a warrior's philosophy:
1. **Virtue** as the foundation of honorable combat
2. **Conquest** as the purpose of martial action
3. **Glory** as the reward earned through deeds
4. **Death** as the acceptable price
