# Arven Doyne

> *"The black ones took them all. I alone crawled back from the water. But something... something stayed behind in the fog."*

## Basics

## Description

Arven Doyne presented himself to the authorities of Port Valdris with hands trembling and eyes that could not stop scanning the shadows. His account—later transcribed by a magistrate's clerk—was delivered in fragments, the same details repeating with small variations, as if his memory had been damaged by what he witnessed.

Physically, Doyne was unremarkable: middle height, weathered features common to sailors, unremarkable clothing now stained and torn. What made him memorable was his *presence*. Those who interviewed him reported an inability to look directly at him, a sense that he was still partially *elsewhere*, still half-drowning in black water.

His eyes, when they focused, showed the particular emptiness of someone who has seen too much. This was not the blank shock of immediate trauma but the settled knowledge of someone who understands they have glimpsed something that cannot be unseen.

### Post-Expedition Life

After giving his account, Doyne vanished from institutional care. Reports place him in various port cities, always near water, always at the cheapest docks where he could hear the sound of waves. He never sailed again. He never went inland. He sleeps with lights burning.

Those who know his story recognize him by his habits: he counts exits, he avoids fog, and he speaks to no one about the black shapes in the water.

## The Account

Doyne's transcribed testimony covers the full arc of the Verdance disaster:

1. **The Storm** — A supernatural tempest that drove a searching vessel onto unknown shores
2. **The Bog Bay** — Black water, twisted trees, toxic mist, no drinkable water
3. **The Disappearances** — One crew member after another vanishing to darkness or drowning
4. **The Attack** — Black figures rising from water, seizing survivors with clawed hands
5. **The Ruins** — Timeless stone structures, sweating and alive, beyond natural architecture
6. **The Ritual** — Blood sacrifice on a floating stone altar; the impalement of a living elf
7. **The Escape** — Two survivors fled through fog while creatures pursued
8. **The Transformation** — A companion caught by black hands; blackening skin before death

The account ends abruptly—Doyne could not continue after describing the moment he heard his companion scream in the water.

## The Question of Credibility

Magistrates and scholars have debated Doyne's testimony for years:

| Argument For | Argument Against |
|--------------|------------------|
| Multiple independent details match other partial accounts | No surviving evidence from the Verdance itself |
| Doyne shows no motive to fabricate | His sanity was clearly compromised |
| Physical evidence (his trauma, his habits) | Other survivors were never found |
| Consistent with fringe reports from the distant territories | Geographic impossibility—where exactly? |

Most scholars land on *uncertain*: Doyne believes everything he says, but whether what he saw was real, fever-dream, or something between remains unproven.

## For Writers / Game Designers

### Themes to Explore

- **Memory as evidence** — Is testimony from trauma reliable? Does it matter if it's *true* or merely *believed*?
- **Survivor's guilt** — Doyne escaped. His companion did not. The fog still calls his name.
- **The cost of knowledge** — Some things cannot be unknown once seen

### Story Hooks

- Doyne appears in the party's city, begging someone to understand what happened to him
- A collector seeks Doyne's testimony—and the price for his silence
- Doyne knows something about the expedition's *original mission* that he hasn't told anyone
- Someone has found the bog bay. They want Doyne to guide them back.
- The Juuhroni have followed him. They're in the harbor now.
- A scholar hires the party to *find* the bog bay while Doyne serves as reluctant guide

### World-Building Notes

- Doyne can serve as a reliable narrator *of his own experience* while remaining unreliable about what it means
- His fragmented, repetitive testimony mirrors real accounts of trauma survivors—useful for authenticity
- The transformation detail gives Doyne a ticking clock: is *he* changing too, more slowly? Has he been a Juuhroni this whole time, just not knowing it?
