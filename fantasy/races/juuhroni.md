## Description

In the distant reaches where no human, elf, or Naterian expansion has yet left its mark, there dwells a people that defy easy classification. The Juuhroni appear in scattered explorer accounts as figures of nightmare—black shapes moving through perpetual mist, their forms indistinguishable from the waters and fogs they call home.

### Physical Appearance

Their skin is not merely dark but *smolista*—tar-like, perpetually moist, as if coated in the black waters of their domain. It glistens without reflecting light, swallowing illumination rather than bouncing it back. Their eyes are black even by human standards, witnesses claim, darker than their already-shadowless skin—windows into something that absorbs rather than perceives.

Their teeth are the most visible betrayal of their predatory nature. Passing from orange to blood-red, fang-like and sharp, they grin with an eagerness that freezes the blood of anyone who sees it. The edge of their features carries the same lethal precision—a blade's edge, a knife's point, angles designed for cutting rather than conversation.

Witnesses universally describe an *upiornego uroku*—a terrible, hypnotic allure. Even as fear grips the heart, something in their movements holds the eye. They flow rather than walk, their damp forms impossibly silent.

### The Drowning Folk

Unlike surface dwellers, the Juuhroni show no fear of deep water. They rise from it, command it, and return to it freely. Their victims speak of being dragged toward the black depths, of hands—always cold, always damp—that grip with inhuman strength.

## Known Encounters

### The Verdance Incident

The most detailed account comes from [[characters/arven-doyne|Arven Doyne]], a survivor of a merchant galley expedition. The *Verdance* was searching for a lost spice ship when a catastrophic storm drove it onto the shores of a nameless bog-bay. The crew, desperate for fresh water, ventured inland and found only death.

> *"The mokradła were black and grew enormous trees, often leaning on others or deformed and half-rotted. Above the water reaching to our waists hung strange vapors, similar to fog but denser, more sinister."*

Over the following days, crew members vanished—one by one, taken by the darkness or suffocated by the toxic mists. When the survivors finally fled toward their remaining ships, black shapes emerged from the water. The creatures seized the living with clawed hands, dragging them into the depths.

Only three survived the initial assault: two humans and an elf. All three were later taken during a ritual sacrifice at the black ruins.

### The Ruins

Doyne described structures of impossible appearance: black ruins built from stone that seemed to *sweat*, ancient as time yet somehow fresh, as if constructed yesterday. Light itself seemed reluctant to touch this place. Without sun, day and night lost meaning—the ruins exist in a perpetual, timeless twilight.

The creatures' altar stood at the base of descending steps rising from black water. Above it floated a dark, irregular stone—narrow but tall, hovering without visible support.

### The Ritual

The surviving witnesses observed a blood cult of horrifying precision. The creatures moved their sacrificial victim—naked, wounded, bleeding—to the altar. Their black claws, when touching bare flesh, seemed to *burn*, draining blood from wounds directly into their grip. The blood traveled *outward* from the injury, not from it, as if pulled against nature.

A priestess—distinguished by her black blade, black as everything else—drove her weapon through the victim's chest and into the abdomen, spilling viscera. The remaining survivors, addled by fear and poison, fled.

### The Touch of Transformation

One survivor, Doyne's companion, was caught by grasping black hands. Though he escaped, his scratched arms had already begun to blacken—showing signs of transformation into one of them. He requested burial at sea rather than risk whatever fate awaited inside him. His companions complied.

This transformation aspect troubles all subsequent readers of Doyne's account. Does contact with Juuhroni begin a change? Can it be reversed? And what, exactly, does one become?

## Religion & Practices

### The Floating Stone

Central to observed Juuhroni worship is a floating black stone at the water's edge. It is irregular, narrow, and tall—and hovers without apparent cause. The creatures treat it with religious fervor, approaching on their knees, prostrating, and conducting elaborate rituals in its presence.

**Hypothesis:** The stone may be connected to [[otchłań|the Abyss]] or other dimensional anomalies. Its anti-gravity properties suggest magical or divine origin. Further investigation is impossible without surviving contact.

### Blood Sacrifice

The Juuhroni appear to require living blood for their rituals. The process—draining from wounds by touch, impalement at the altar, general reverence—suggests a structured belief system, not mere predation. They may believe they are *feeding* the stone, the water, or some entity within both.

### The Nameless Bay

Doyne's account places these events at a "bagnista zatoka"—a bog bay—on the far edges of known waters. No cartographer has successfully returned with maps. Whether this is due to the territory's hostility, its victims' deaths, or some geographic impossibility remains unknown.

## For Writers / Game Designers

### Themes to Explore

- **Transformation and identity** — What happens when touch begins changing you? When your body betrays your soul?
- **The terror of the unknown** — The Juuhroni represent fears beyond understanding: drowning, darkness, being watched from black water
- **Cultures beyond contact** — What beliefs develop in isolation? What rituals evolved without outside influence?
- **Blood and sacrifice** — The oldest gods require the oldest offerings

### Story Hooks

- A survivor arrives at port with blackened arms and an impossible story
- A lost expedition's logs reveal they've found the bog bay—and entered it willingly
- Someone seeks the Juuhroni deliberately, perhaps hoping to become one, or to bargain with them
- The floating stone appears in a different location—has something *moved*?
- A ship wanders into the bay by accident; the party must navigate the fog and escape
- A Juuhroni specimen (alive? dead? transformed?) arrives in a city—housed in a temple? Locked in a prison? Freed by cultists?

### World-Building Notes

- The Juuhroni can serve as a source of horror beyond comprehension—creatures that *swim from water and阴影*, that drain blood through untouched skin
- The transformation aspect suggests a deeper connection between the Juuhroni and their environment—are they former humans? Refugees from catastrophe? Something else entirely?
- Their distant habitat makes them useful as a threat that cannot simply be *defeated*—they exist beyond reach, and the danger is them *reaching toward you*
- The floating stone raises questions: is it the Juuhroni's god? A dimensional anchor? A prison for something that the blood sacrifices keep sealed?
