# Orzekacz Naterian (Naterian Adjudicator)

*[Judicial Authority, Nater Hegemony]*

> *"I am law. I am verdict. I am execution."*
> — Saying attributed to Adjudicators

## Overview

| Attribute | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| **Category** | Judicial Faction/Role |
| **Territory** | Nater Hegemony |
| **Type** | Mobile one-person tribunals |
| **Alignment** | Lawful Neutral to Lawful Evil |

## Description

The Adjudicators are the most feared figures in the Nater Hegemony. Each operates alone, traveling the roads and settlements of the Hegemony as a living embodiment of the state's authority. They are judge, jury, and executioner—answerable only to the Hegemon themselves.

An Adjudicator carries three things: a black iron medallion bearing the Hegemon's seal, a ledger of laws thick as a man's fist, and a weapon of their choosing. In practice, the weapon is often ceremonial—the Adjudicator rarely needs to fight. The mere announcement of their arrival is enough to stop townsfolk in their tracks.

### Authority

The power of an Adjudicator is nearly absolute:

| Power | Scope |
|-------|-------|
| **Immediate Verdict** | Can sentence any crime on the spot |
| **Execution** | May execute sentences without trial or appeal |
| **Seizure** | Can confiscate property, arrest any citizen |
| **Exemption** | Cannot be obstructed, harmed, or questioned by common folk |

The only authority above an Adjudicator is the Hegemon. Even nobles and military commanders defer to them—if reluctantly. To defy an Adjudicator is to defy the state itself.

### Methods

Adjudicators are methodical. They observe before they act, listening to whispers and watching behavior. A traveler who cheats a merchant, a landlord who overcharges the desperate, a parent who beats a child in the street—all are noted. Then, without warning, the Adjudicator speaks.

Their sentences tend toward the swift and final. Theft earns the loss of the stolen hand. Murder earns death. Sedition earns a slow, public end that serves as warning to others. Mercy exists, but it is rare and always calculated—the Adjudicator must believe rehabilitation serves the Hegemony better than punishment.

## Origins

The position emerged during the Consolidation Wars, when the Hegemon needed eyes and hands in every corner of the new state. Regular courts were too slow, too corrupt, too susceptible to local pressures. The Adjudicators solved this. They are outsiders by design—known to no community, loyal to no faction, bound only to the law as interpreted by the Hegemon.

## Legends and Rumors

Few speak openly about Adjudicators, but whispers persist:

- **They cannot be killed by ordinary means.** Some say they are protected by dark pacts. Others claim they simply carry so much legal authority that reality itself resists their death. Most likely: anyone who kills an Adjudicator becomes the entire Hegemony's next target.

- **They meet in secret every decade.** In a place no outsider has found, all Adjudicators gather to synchronize their methods and receive new directives. What is discussed there filters down as policy changes within a year.

- **The Hegemon names them from the condemned.** It is said that those who show exceptional ruthlessness in prison, those who kill guards and take up arms against their captors, are sometimes offered a choice: death, or service as an Adjudicator. Most accept. Survival trumps honor.

- **They fear one thing: a fair trial.** If an Adjudicator could be brought before proper tribunal—prosecuted for overreach—their power would shatter. No such tribunal exists under Hegemony law. This is not an accident.

## For Writers / Game Designers

### Themes to Explore
- **Justice vs. tyranny** — Is the Hegemony's harsh order better than chaos?
- **Institutional power** — Can any single person be trusted with absolute authority?
- **Fear as governance** — Does the threat of an Adjudicator prevent crime, or merely delay it?

### Story Hooks
- A party member commits a minor crime in the Hegemony; rumors say an Adjudicator is nearby
- A village harbors someone the Adjudicator seeks—innocent or guilty becomes irrelevant
- An Adjudicator begins questioning the Hegemon's authority, becoming an enemy of the state
- A murdered Adjudicator is found; the Hegemony demands answers, but someone powerful wants the truth buried

### World-Building Notes
- Adjudicators work well as antagonists who are technically "just" but horrifying in practice
- Can serve as reluctant allies if the party's goals align with Hegemony law
- The mystery of their origins and true nature adds spice—what ARE they?
