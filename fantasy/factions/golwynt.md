# Golwynt

> "Szponami do kości." *(Claws to the bone.)*

| Field | Value |

## Description

The house of Golwynt rules the fertile valleys and rolling farmlands that feed the entire realm. While other houses compete for trade routes and magical artifacts, Golwynt simply grows wheat — endless fields of it, enough to feed armies and cities alike.

They are often overlooked in political machinations, dismissed as "mere farmers" by the scheming houses. This underestimation is a fatal mistake. Golwynt's wealth is steady, their power is fundamental, and their patience is agricultural — measured in seasons, not days.

### The Breadbasket

Golwynt territory produces:

| Resource | Annual Yield | Notes |
|----------|--------------|-------|
| Wheat | 80% of realm's total | Surplus exported |
| Barley | 60% of realm's total | Beer brewing |
| Vegetables | 70% of realm's total | Market gardens |
| Livestock | 50% of realm's total | Beef, wool, leather |

This gives Golwynt a power no one admits: *they can starve cities into submission*.

### The Harvest Mark

All Golwynt lords bear a distinctive mark — a clawed handprint in harvest gold, branded during their majority. The origin is disputed:
- Some say it honors an ancient pact with earth spirits
- Others claim it marks those who "dug their way to lordship" (literal serf ancestors)
- A few whisper it signifies their half-beast heritage

Whatever the truth, the mark is both pride and shame — proof of Golwynt's rise from nothing.

## For Writers / Game Designers

### Themes to Explore
- The power of overlooked people
- Slow and steady versus quick and clever
- Class and legitimacy
- The earth as a source of power

### Story Hooks
- A drought threatens the harvest — someone is sabotaging the water supply
- Lord Harren seeks a marriage alliance that no one expected
- Graz wants to buy exclusive grain rights — permanently
- A peasant uprising threatens the lords — Golwynt must choose sides

### GM Notes
> Golwynt makes an interesting patron — they seem weak but control survival itself. They are also sympathetic; their "rise from nothing" story resonates with common folk.

## Trivia

- Golwynt's harvest festival draws visitors from across the realm
- Their knights fight with farming tools made deadly — pitchforks, scythes
- A saying: "The south may forget its gods, but never its bread"
- Golwynt serfs have more rights than most houses' peasants
