> "Waleczność ogara." *(The ferocity of the hound.)*

## Description

The house of Skjor controls the only reliable trade route between the human realm and the dwarven kingdoms of the deep mountains. This makes them indispensable — and rich. Their seat, Ponury Zamek (Grim Castle), sits at the mouth of a vast underground passage that eventually leads to the dwarven realms.

Unlike most humans, Skjor has actual *friendships* with dwarves — relationships built over generations of trade and mutual respect. This connection gives them access to dwarven craftsmanship, metals, and knowledge unavailable anywhere else.

### The Undergate

The underground passage — called *Podbramie* (Undergate) — is Skjor's greatest asset:

| Aspect | Description |
|--------|-------------|
| Length | Unknown — no human has reached the end |
| Width | Wide enough for loaded ore carts |
| Features | Lighting stations every mile (dwarven torches) |
| Dangers | Deep-things, cave-ins, lost souls |
| Control | Skjor guards all entrances |

### Dwarven Relations

Skjor has maintained the *"Przymierze Kamienia"* (Stone Alliance) with the dwarves for three centuries. Key aspects:
- Annual tribute of human luxury goods (wine, textiles, spices)
- Dwarven craftsmen operate openly in Skjor territory
- Joint defense of the Undergate
- Shared intelligence on threats from below

## For Writers / Game Designers

### Themes to Explore
- Cross-cultural friendship
- The tension between human ambition and dwarven tradition
- What lies beneath?
- The value of genuine alliances

### Story Hooks
- Something is attacking caravans in the Undergate — party hired to investigate
- The dwarves are sealing portions of the Undergate — why?
- A human wants to establish competing trade routes
- Deep-things have been seen — ancient horrors awakening

### GM Notes
> Skjor makes an excellent patron for underground adventures and provides access to dwarven resources. Their half-dwarf lord creates interesting cultural tensions.

## Trivia

- Skjor nobility often have dwarven features — braided hair, broad builds, deep voices
- Their castles are built half-above, half-below ground
- The phrase "Skjor's word" means absolute reliability in trade
- Some houses resent their dwarven connection — "too close to the mountain folk"
