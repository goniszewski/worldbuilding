# Merchant Tides

> A fleet of ledgers, sails, and smiling agreements that survives by never committing more deeply than profit requires.

## Overview

| Field | Value |
|-------|-------|
| **Type** | Mercantile Coalition |
| **Character** | Polished, pragmatic, calculating |
| **Scale** | Coastal and interregional |
| **Status** | Active, wealthy, internally competitive |
| **Center of Gravity** | [[places/verath|Verath]] and allied harbors |

## Description

Merchant Tides is less a nation than an understanding between rich people who know war is bad for shipping schedules. The coalition is made up of captains, counting houses, insurers, dock families, and warehouse syndicates that have agreed, more or less, that commerce deserves protection from noble feuds and ideological crusades.

They present themselves as neutral custodians of exchange. In practice, they are masters of selective neutrality. Merchant Tides will trade with almost anyone, but never without quietly measuring risk, leverage, and future obligation. They do not seek crowns. They seek the ability to outlast those who do.

## Internal Order

| Body | Role |
|------|------|
| **Council of Captains** | Public leadership and policy disputes |
| **Harbor Houses** | Control warehousing, tariffs, and labor in specific ports |
| **Letter Brokers** | Manage contracts, credit, and sensitive correspondence |
| **Silent Partners** | Investors whose names rarely appear in public |

The coalition is strongest when trade is threatened. In peacetime, rivalries sharpen and every member remembers that shared prosperity is still competition.

## Interests

Merchant Tides is driven by a few practical goals:
- Keep sea lanes and ports functioning
- Prevent any one state or faction from controlling all maritime trade
- Profit from scarcity without letting scarcity destroy the market
- Gather information early enough to turn crisis into advantage

They often know about unrest, shortages, or coups before rulers do. Their ships carry goods, but they also carry rumors, witnesses, fugitives, and letters that should never have survived the crossing.

## Reputation

To nobles, Merchant Tides are useful but untrustworthy. To common sailors, they are often the closest thing the sea has to law. To revolutionaries and smugglers, they are either indispensable allies or the sort of people who will sell your location if the price is right.

Their greatest strength is not wealth alone, but plausibility. Everyone believes they need the merchants. The merchants work hard to keep that belief true.

## For Story Use

### Themes to Explore
- Neutrality as performance
- Wealth as a form of soft power
- Trade networks that become political actors

### Story Hooks
- A merchant convoy disappears after carrying sealed arcane cargo
- The Council of Captains fractures over whether to back a rebellion
- A port ledger contains proof that one house funded both sides of a conflict

### Adaptation Notes
- Merchant Tides work well as an unreliable stabilizing force
- They should feel too practical to love and too necessary to ignore
