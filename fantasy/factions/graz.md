## Description

The house of Graz is the youngest of the great noble houses, having risen from successful merchants to landed aristocracy within living memory. Unlike the ancient houses whose power comes from bloodlines or military might, Graz's influence stems from a simple truth: *money makes the world turn*.

Founded by **Baron Aldric Graz**, a shipping magnate who financed the Crown during the *War of Insolvency*, the house now controls Redwood — the realm's second-greatest trading hub. Their warehouses overflow with goods from across the known world, and their gold backs the loans that keep lesser nobles solvent.

### The New Nobility

Graz represents a new kind of power in the realm — one based not on swords or ancient blood, but on commerce and connections. This makes them simultaneously respected (for their wealth) and resented (for their origins).

Their rise has created tension with old nobility:
- *"Graz bought their titles; we earned ours."*
- *"Their gold is good, but their honor is negotiable."*

### Commercial Empire

Graz controls:
- The Redwood Trade Fair (annual, largest in the realm)
- Three merchant fleets
- Banking houses in seven cities
- The most extensive information network outside the Czar spy network

## For Writers / Game Designers

### Themes to Explore
- Is money a corrupting influence, or a neutral tool?
- The tension between old and new power structures
- What can't be bought?
- The loneliness of wealth

### Story Hooks
- A party needs funding for a quest — Graz will lend, but at what price?
- Someone is sabotaging Graz's trade routes
- Lord Valdric seeks to marry into an old house, but the family resists
- A Graz merchant has gone missing with sensitive documents

### GM Notes
> Graz makes excellent quest-givers for parties seeking wealth and status. They are honest in their transactions but ruthless in protecting their interests.

## Trivia

- Graz nobles are the only ones who routinely acknowledge commoners by name
- The Graz family has a tradition: no family member may inherit without spending one year as a common merchant
- Their motto is sometimes mocked: *"Everything has its price"* — but they've never broken a contract
